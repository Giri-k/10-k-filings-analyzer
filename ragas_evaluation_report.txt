RAGAS Evaluation Report — 10-K Filings RAG Pipeline
====================================================

1. What is RAGAS?
-----------------
RAGAS (Retrieval Augmented Generation Assessment) is a framework for
evaluating RAG pipelines. Instead of requiring hand-labeled ground truth
for every query, it uses an LLM-as-judge approach: a language model scores
the quality of retrieval and generation along several dimensions. This makes
it practical to evaluate pipelines where collecting human annotations is
expensive or infeasible — such as domain-specific financial document QA.

We used RAGAS v0.4.3 with Ollama (llama3.1) as the judge LLM and
all-MiniLM-L6-v2 for embedding-based relevancy scoring.


2. Metrics Used (All Reference-Free)
-------------------------------------
We evaluated on four metrics, none of which require pre-written ground
truth answers:

a) Faithfulness (F)
   Measures whether every claim in the generated answer is supported by
   the retrieved context. The judge LLM extracts individual claims from
   the answer, then checks each one against the retrieved chunks.
   Score 1.0 = every claim is grounded; 0.0 = none are.

b) Answer Relevancy (AR)
   Measures whether the answer actually addresses the question asked.
   RAGAS generates N synthetic questions from the answer, embeds them
   alongside the original question, and computes cosine similarity.
   A high score means the answer stays on-topic; a low score means
   it drifts or discusses unrelated content.

c) Context Precision (CP)
   Measures whether the retrieved chunks are relevant to the question.
   The judge LLM examines each retrieved chunk and determines if it
   contains information useful for answering the query, then computes
   a precision-weighted score (relevant chunks ranked higher score better).
   This evaluates the retrieval pipeline, not the generator.

d) Context Utilization (CU)
   Measures how well the retrieved context is used. Similar to context
   precision but focused on whether the answer leverages the relevant
   chunks that were provided. A high score means the pipeline is not
   wasting good context; a low score means relevant chunks were retrieved
   but ignored during generation.


3. Evaluation Setup
-------------------
- Ticker evaluated: AAPL (Apple Inc.)
- Filings: 5 years of 10-K annual reports (2021–2025)
- Indexed chunks: 42,118 chunks (1000 chars each, 200 overlap)
- Retrieval: Hybrid BM25 + dense cosine similarity, top-20 candidates
  merged, cross-encoder reranked to top-5
- Generator: Ollama llama3.1 (8B parameters, local inference)
- Judge LLM: Same Ollama llama3.1 instance
- Embeddings: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384-dim sentence embeddings)
- Test set: 10 diverse financial queries covering risk factors, revenue,
  supply chain, competition, regulation, R&D, legal, outlook, cybersecurity,
  and debt/liquidity

Test Queries:
  1. What are the company's major risk factors?
  2. What does management discuss about revenue trends?
  3. What are the key supply chain risks?
  4. What does the company say about competition?
  5. What are the main regulatory risks?
  6. What does the company report about research and development?
  7. What are the significant legal proceedings?
  8. What does management say about future outlook?
  9. What are the cybersecurity and data privacy risks?
  10. What are the company's debt and liquidity positions?

5. Analysis and Insights
------------------------
RETRIEVAL QUALITY IS STRONG (93.7% context precision/utilization):
  The hybrid retrieval pipeline (BM25 + dense similarity + cross-encoder
  reranker) consistently surfaces relevant chunks. 9 out of 10 queries
  achieved perfect 1.0 context precision. The one outlier was "revenue
  trends" (0.367), likely because revenue discussion is spread across
  multiple sections (MD&A, financial statements, risk factors) rather
  than concentrated under a single ITEM heading, making it harder for
  the retrieval pipeline to find the most targeted chunks.

FAITHFULNESS IS MODERATE WITH HIGH VARIANCE (64.9% ±29.3%):
  The generator (llama3.1-8B) sometimes adds claims not present in the
  retrieved context. This is the classic hallucination problem in RAG.
  - High faithfulness (1.0): R&D, Competition — these topics have
    concentrated, factual content in specific 10-K sections.
  - Low faithfulness (0.17–0.20): Regulatory risks, Revenue trends —
    the LLM likely relies on its parametric knowledge of Apple rather
    than strictly the retrieved context, producing plausible but
    ungrounded statements.
  - Mitigation: Stronger prompt instructions ("answer ONLY based on the
    provided context"), or using a larger model that follows instructions
    more reliably.

ANSWER RELEVANCY IS MODERATE (65.9% ±24.5%):
  The answers sometimes drift from the specific question asked.
  - Lowest score: Legal proceedings (0.0) — the generator likely
    produced a generic response rather than addressing the specific
    legal proceedings question, or the answer was too short/vague
    for RAGAS to generate meaningful synthetic questions.
  - Highest score: Cybersecurity (0.882) — a focused, specific topic
    where the answer stayed tightly on-topic.

RETRIEVAL VS GENERATION GAP:
  The ~30-point gap between retrieval scores (93.7%) and generation
  scores (~65%) tells a clear story: our retrieval pipeline is doing
  its job well, but the generation step is the bottleneck. The right
  chunks are being found, but the LLM is not always using them
  faithfully or answering the question directly.

NOTE ON EVALUATION METHODOLOGY:
  Using the same model (llama3.1-8B) as both generator and judge is a
  limitation. The judge may be too lenient or share the same biases as
  the generator. In a production setting, using a stronger model
  (e.g., GPT-4, Claude) as the judge would give more reliable scores.
  However, for a local, cost-free evaluation, this approach still
  surfaces meaningful quality signals and relative differences across
  query types.


6. Generation Improvements and Re-Evaluation
----------------------------------------------
After analyzing the baseline results, we identified three root causes of
low faithfulness and applied targeted prompt engineering fixes:

ROOT CAUSES IDENTIFIED:
  a) No grounding constraint — the system prompt said "Never make up
     financial information" but didn't instruct the model to answer ONLY
     from retrieved context.
  b) 40% of each chunk was discarded — _execute_search() truncated each
     1000-char chunk to 600 chars (doc[:600]), silently dropping the tail
     of every chunk which often contains key figures or conclusions.
  c) Weak forced-answer prompt — when the agent exhausted its ReAct steps,
     it was told "Provide your Final Answer now" with no grounding rules.

CHANGES APPLIED:
  a) Rewrote SYSTEM_PROMPT with explicit grounding rules:
     "Base your Final Answer ONLY on information from search results.
      Do NOT add facts, figures, or claims from your own knowledge."
  b) Removed 600-char truncation — full 1000-char chunks are ~250 tokens
     each; 5 chunks = ~1250 tokens, trivial for llama3.1's 128K context.
  c) Added numbered source labels ([Source 1: AAPL 2024 - ITEM_1A]) and
     trailing grounding reminders at the end of every search observation.
  d) Strengthened the forced final-answer prompt with citation and
     grounding instructions.
  e) Updated evaluation prompt to match the improved grounding philosophy.

KEY FINDINGS:
  The grounding constraints had the largest impact on queries where the
  model was most prone to hallucination:
  - Regulatory risks: 0.167 → 0.875 (+424%) — the model previously added
    regulatory details from parametric knowledge; now stays grounded.
  - Revenue trends: 0.200 → 0.750 (+275%) — previously supplemented with
    general Apple revenue knowledge; now only cites retrieved excerpts.
  - Legal proceedings: 0.600 → 1.000 (+67%) — fully grounded answer.

  Answer Relevancy scores are unreliable: 5/10 queries scored AR=0.000,
  which are judge failures (llama3.1-8B fails to generate valid synthetic
  questions for the AR metric). This is a known limitation of using a
  small model as the RAGAS judge.

  Context Precision/Utilization remained strong, confirming the retrieval
  pipeline is unaffected by the generation prompt changes.

LIMITATION — JUDGE MODEL NOISE:
  Using llama3.1-8B as the RAGAS judge introduces significant noise. The
  model is too small to consistently perform the structured reasoning
  required by RAGAS metrics (claim extraction, synthetic question
  generation). A stronger judge model (GPT-4, Claude) would produce more
  reliable and consistent scores. Despite this noise, the per-query
  improvements on hallucination-prone topics are clear and directionally
  correct.


7. Challenges Faced Deploying RAGAS
-----------------------------------
RAGAS v0.4.3 presented several integration challenges:

a) API Inconsistency Between Old and New Metrics:
   RAGAS 0.4.3 has two metric APIs that don't fully interoperate:
   - Old API (ragas.metrics): Pre-instantiated metric singletons
     (e.g., faithfulness, answer_relevancy) that work with evaluate(),
     but context_precision requires a "reference" column (human-written
     ground truth) that we don't have.
   - New API (ragas.metrics.collections): Proper classes like
     ContextPrecisionWithoutReference that don't need ground truth,
     but they inherit from SimpleBaseMetric instead of Metric, so the
     evaluate() function rejects them with a TypeError.
   Solution: We bypassed evaluate() entirely and called metric.score()
   directly on each new-API metric, building our own evaluation loop.

b) Deprecated LangChain Wrappers:
   The documented approach of using LangchainLLMWrapper with ChatOllama
   was deprecated. RAGAS 0.4.3 introduced llm_factory() which requires
   an OpenAI-compatible client. Ollama exposes an OpenAI-compatible
   endpoint at /v1, so we used the openai Python package pointing to
   Ollama's local server.

c) Sync vs Async Client Mismatch:
   The new metrics internally call ascore() (async), which requires an
   AsyncOpenAI client. Using a synchronous OpenAI client raised
   "Cannot use agenerate() with a synchronous client." We had to switch
   from OpenAI() to AsyncOpenAI() for the evaluator LLM.

d) HuggingFace Embeddings Class Name Case Sensitivity:
   ragas.embeddings exports both HuggingfaceEmbeddings (lowercase 'f')
   and HuggingFaceEmbeddings (uppercase 'F'). The lowercase version is
   an abstract class that cannot be instantiated. Only the uppercase
   HuggingFaceEmbeddings works.

e) Token Limit Issues with Local Judge:
   Ollama's default max_tokens sometimes truncates the judge's output
   during claim extraction for faithfulness scoring. One query (supply
   chain risks) failed because the generated answer was long enough that
   extracting and verifying all claims exceeded the token budget. A
   larger context window or chunked evaluation would fix this.

f) Dataset Column Naming:
   RAGAS 0.4.3 changed the expected column names from "question" /
   "answer" / "contexts" to "user_input" / "response" /
   "retrieved_contexts". Using the old names silently produces errors
   about missing required columns.

g) Output Buffering:
   Python's default stdout buffering meant evaluation progress was
   invisible when running as a background process. Required
   PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1 to see real-time progress.
